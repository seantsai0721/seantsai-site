<Section n="01" label="Site" title="A forgotten path to the Grand Hotel.">

The site is the 823 Artillery Battle Memorial Park in Taipei's Zhongshan District, at the foot of Jiantan Mountain, on the Keelung River and beside the Grand Hotel.

People once walked straight from the Meiji Bridge up to the Taiwan Shrine on the hill, and the approach was laid out to feel solemn and ceremonial. As the city grew, elevated roads and a tangle of new streets erased that landscape, and today no walking route to the hotel survives. The bridge itself, finished in 1914 beside an English-style garden, was once one of the most scenic crossings in Taipei. Older residents still remember playing under it and swimming in the pool nearby. After the war it was marked for demolition. From 1968 its stone balustrades and lamp posts were removed and it was widened, and the Zhongshan Expressway and Xinsheng viaduct were later built overhead, blocking the views of mountain and river that once defined the place.

<Section n="02" label="Concept" title="Bring back Taipei's city of mountains and water.">

An elevated walkway through the park reconnects the main approach to the hotel. Walking between mountain, river and city, visitors see a new view at each step, recovering the old idea of Taipei as a "shanshui" city of mountains and water. The design combines four systems: walking access, visual access, ecological access and urban resilience.

<Section n="04" label="Walking access" title="Gentle, quiet and easy to rest on.">

Along the park edge, small changes in ground level lift the walkway gradually and screen it from the heavy traffic noise on Bei'an Road. At each viewpoint the walkway widens and flattens, with simple seats and handrails along the railing so people can stop and take in the view. Under the middle of the walkway, an earth-covered accessible public toilet keeps built structures from intruding on the habitat.

<Section n="05" label="Ecological access" title="Habitat from the hill to the water.">

The 30-metre passage links the Jiantan Mountain and Keelung River ecosystems, widening the range of habitats in the city. Inside the park, ground shaped to different depths makes room for dry land, waterside wetland and open water, each with its own birds, amphibians, insects and fish. A sandstone stepping-stone path lets visitors walk into the wetland without disturbing it.
