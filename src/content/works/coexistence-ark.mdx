<Section n="01" label="Site" title="A creek that lost its landscape twice.">

Guizikeng Creek runs 5.2 km down from the hills of southern Taishan to join Dakeng Creek in the Tamsui River system. In 1969 the plain around it was rice paddy. By 2002 about 80% of that farmland had become factories, and decades of industrial and household discharge left the creek slow, polluted and foul-smelling.

Factories are now being cleared for the Wuguzi redevelopment district. The plan leaves only scattered park lots for green space, beside a new arterial road and an airport MRT line where noise reaches around 100 dB.

</Section>

<Section n="02" label="Strategy" title="Three reaches, three kinds of nature.">

Ecological surveys of the upstream catchment showed three systems meeting along the creek: low-hill forest, urban habitat and wetland. Each reach of the corridor is designed around the one closest to it.

<Section n="03" label="Core park" title="Wetland in the middle, paddies around it, forest at the edge.">

The core park in Reach 1 is laid out as an ecological patch. A wetland core of shallow pools and mudflats sits at the centre, sized from at least 0.5 ha for amphibians and small birds toward the 3–5 ha small mammals need. Terraced rice paddies wrap it as a buffer, and a layered low-hill woodland lines the outer edge.

The large slope collects surface runoff into a cistern. After settling, the water flows by gravity to the indoor rice farm below, so irrigation uses almost no pumping energy. The building covers 15% of the park, under an earth-covered roof.

</Section>

<Section n="05" label="Building" title="A building that farms rice and absorbs the train.">

Under the planted slope, four tiers of indoor paddies grow rice under LED light. Controlled light, temperature and nutrients cut the growing cycle from about five months to 60–70 days, so each tier yields five harvests a year at roughly 0.9 kg per square metre.

The facade facing the airport MRT is a deep honeycomb of tapered cells. Train noise sits mostly between 50 and 200 Hz, wavelengths of 6.86 to 1.72 m, and a cavity a quarter of a wavelength deep traps it. Cell depths run from 1.72 m down to 0.30 m, absorbing fully across 50–285 Hz and lowering noise by about 20–30 dB.

<Section n="06" label="Ecology" title="Designing for the food web, from dragonflies to the crested serpent eagle.">

Planting is chosen for each of the three habitat types and for the species it supports at every level: insects and frogs such as the Taipei tree frog at the base, birds like the Taiwan blue magpie and white-eye in the middle, and egrets, kingfishers and the crested serpent eagle at the top.

</Section>
