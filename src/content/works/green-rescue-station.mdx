<Section n="01" label="Brief" title="Medical help that arrives by air, in the containers already on the plane.">

Natural disasters, conflict and disease outbreaks are becoming more frequent, and the places hit hardest are often the hardest to reach. Field hospitals need to arrive fast, set up anywhere and grow or shrink with the situation.

The brief asked how an airline could put its own assets to work for sustainability. Air cargo containers, known as unit load devices, are light, strong, built to fit an aircraft hold and already move around the world every day. This proposal turns them into the rooms of a temporary hospital: an X-ray room in one container, an operating room in two, a ward assembled from containers, cargo pallets and a transparent tarpaulin.

</Section>

<Section n="05" label="Energy" title="Running on its own power.">

Solar panels on the roof power the station during the day, and surplus energy is stored as backup alongside the generator. Stacked storage containers at the end of the ward hold drinking water and food, so the station can keep running before regular supply lines are restored.

<Figure variant="wide" src="/works/green-rescue-station/energy.webp" alt="Section through the ward, 19.21 m long, with beds, IV stands and a patient, a stacked storage unit at one end and an energy storage unit at the other" caption="Energy system section, 1:150" />

</Section>
